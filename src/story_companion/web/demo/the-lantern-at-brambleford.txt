The Lantern at Brambleford

CHAPTER 1: THE LETTER

Mara Vale arrived in Brambleford before sunrise, carrying a brass compass
and a hand-drawn atlas tied with green thread. Her aunt's letter contained
only one instruction: meet Tomas Reed beneath the clock tower before the
seventh bell.

Tomas was the village ferryman. He knew every bend of the River Lume and
claimed he could name a landing place by the sound of its water. When Mara
showed him the atlas, he tapped a faded mark beside the eastern bank.

"That path is older than the bridge," Tomas said. "If your aunt used it, she
would have left a lantern sign."

Mara wrote the distance from the tower to the ferry in the margin of her
atlas: one and a quarter miles.

CHAPTER 2: THE RIVER MARKER

At dawn the next day, Mara and Tomas crossed the River Lume in his narrow
blue boat. The current carried them south for nearly half a mile before
Tomas brought the boat against the eastern reeds.

They found a small lantern carved into the stone marker. Mara compared its
three-pointed flame with a sketch in her aunt's letter and decided the marks
had been made by the same hand.

Tomas warned that the north road would take four hours on foot. Mara checked
the light, measured the remaining distance on her atlas, and chose to wait
until morning.

CHAPTER 3: THE NORTH ROAD

The north road climbed beyond the last orchard. At the ridge, Mara discovered
why her aunt had hidden the route and what waited at the ruined observatory.
